Trust · November 22, 2026

The Missing Click.

In Pew’s March 2025 browsing panel, people clicked a traditional result on 8% of Google search visits with an AI summary and 15% without one. A summary link got a click on 1% of visits with a summary.1

How Pew counted clicks

Pew logged 68,879 Google searches by 900 U.S. adults in March. It reran the searches in April to identify the results. A URL found in both places counted as a summary link. The published method does not say how sponsored results were handled or report paid clicks separately. AI summaries appeared more often on long or question-like queries, so the with-summary and without-summary groups also differed in what they looked up.

Answers on the search page

Earlier click studies

In Ahrefs’ 2017 study, the first ordinary result took about 20% of clicks when a featured snippet appeared above it and 26% when there was no snippet. The snippet itself took about 9%. The rank comparison covered 100,000 keywords. A separate comparison across different keywords also found fewer clicks per search on pages with snippets.7

In a June 2019 Jumpshot browser panel, more than half of searches ended without a click.10 A January–April 2026 Similarweb panel put the share at 68% for U.S. browser searches. The samples and device weights differ.11

A randomized test

In a March 2026 field experiment, Wang and colleagues randomly assigned 1,100 U.S. Chrome users to different versions of Google Search. Blocking AI Overviews and AI Mode raised external click-through by 8.8 percentage points relative to standard Search. Making AI Mode the only Google search option lowered it by 18.8 points. The experiment ran for ten days.8

Where the visits go

After Google rolled out AI Overviews, researchers estimated about 5% fewer monthly search referrals to a set of English Wikipedia articles. The estimate depends on whether matched German and French articles provide a good comparison.2

Chartbeat recorded a 34% fall in Google-referred pageviews at its publisher sites between December 2024 and December 2025.13 Google said in August 2025 that total outbound organic clicks held relatively steady year over year. Changes in search volume and the distribution of clicks among sites can produce both patterns. The periods and site coverage also differ.3

Other referral measures

Chartbeat’s average weekly pageviews across traffic sources fell 5.9% from 2024 to 2025.13

In Semrush’s 2025 site sample, AI services such as ChatGPT and Perplexity accounted for an estimated 0.14% of visits across sites, compared with 16% from organic search.9

Why the click matters

A visit gives a publisher the chance to earn from an ad, sign up a reader or receive a correction. Google can earn from a paid click on its own results page. Alphabet’s figures show how those two outcomes can diverge.

In Alphabet’s Q3 2026 filing, Search & other ad revenue rose from $56.6 billion to $63.4 billion, up 12% from a year earlier. Its share of Google advertising rose from 76.3% to 77.2%. Paid clicks on this reporting base grew 8% and cost per click 4%. Alphabet groups Google Search with properties such as Maps, Gmail and Play and gives no separate revenue line for AI Overviews.12

In the annual filings, Search & other rose from $175.0 billion to $224.5 billion between 2023 and 2025. Over the same years Google Network, the ad business on participating sites and apps, fell from $31.3 billion to $29.8 billion, or from 13.2% to 10.1% of Google advertising.14

What one publisher earns from a visit

People Inc. reported that visits to its most significant sites, including brands such as Allrecipes and Investopedia, fell by about a quarter year over year in Q3 2026. Revenue from licensing, syndication and other digital activity outside its own sites helped offset the loss of visit-based revenue.4

Maintaining the sources

For volunteer-run reference sites and specialist guides, a visit can bring someone who adds a missing source or fixes a stale answer. The next question is whether fewer arrivals from search leave fewer people doing that work. A click count alone cannot tell us.
