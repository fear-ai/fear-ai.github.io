Trust · November 22, 2026

AI
Summaries.

Fewer clicks to traditional results.

In a March 2025 browsing panel covering 900 U.S. adults and 68,879 Google searches, Pew found that people opened a traditional result on 8% of search visits with an AI summary, compared with 15% on visits without one. On 1% of visits with a summary, they opened a URL that Pew classified as a summary link. That classification includes URLs also appearing in the traditional results. Pew browsing study1

How Pew counted clicks

Pew reconstructed result pages in April. A URL in both the summary and the traditional results counted as a summary link.

Pew observed browsing in March and reran the searches in April to see which results appeared. Summaries appeared more often for longer and question-like searches. The comparison reflects differences in what people asked as well as what appeared on the page. Pew methods1

Zero-click search

In June 2019, more than half of the searches in a Jumpshot browser panel ended without a click. In January–April 2026, a Similarweb panel put the share at 68.01% for U.S. browser searches. The panels use different samples and device weights. They show that zero-click search was already common years before the newer measurement. 2019 search study7 · 2026 search study8

Public participation was changing too. By Q2 2022, Stack Overflow had 22.6% fewer surviving new public questions than in the same quarter of 2019. That comparison ends before ChatGPT’s public launch in November 2022. Stack Exchange API5 · ChatGPT launch6

Visits to sources

Researchers Mehrzad Khosravi and Hema Yoganarasimhan used the 2024 rollout of Google AI Overviews to compare external-search visits to the same Wikipedia articles in English, German and French. Their October 2026 working paper estimates a drop in monthly English search referrals of 5.45% against the German comparison and 4.82% against the French one. The estimates depend on how well those language editions approximate what would have happened to English traffic without the rollout. They cover visits from search engines to Wikipedia. Wikipedia study, Table 22

On sites tracked by Chartbeat, pageviews referred by Google Search dropped 34% between December 2024 and December 2025. Google had said in August 2025 that its total outbound organic clicks held relatively steady year over year. The two statements cover different periods and sets of sites. More searches can offset fewer clicks per search, and gains for some sites can offset losses elsewhere. Google’s statement gives no breakdown of where the clicks went. Chartbeat, pp. 3–410 · Google Search statement3

Traffic to the sources themselves presents a mixed history. English Wikipedia’s human-classified browser pageviews rose from 2022 to 2024, then fell. In the first ten months of 2026 they were 9.9% below the same months of 2025. The count combines desktop and mobile web and includes all sources of visits. Wikimedia revised its bot detection in 2025, which complicates comparisons over time. At Chartbeat’s publisher sites, average weekly pageviews across traffic sources fell 5.9% from 2024 to 2025. Those losses are specific to Chartbeat’s network and do not isolate what AI caused. Wikimedia data9 · Chartbeat report, pp. 3–410

Visits and revenue

People Inc., the publisher behind brands including Allrecipes and Investopedia, illustrates how different the traffic and business effects can be. Its parent reported that visits to its most significant sites fell 26% year over year in Q3 2026. Digital revenue tied to site visits fell 6%, while other digital revenue rose 15%. Total digital revenue grew 2%. Including print, the segment’s revenue fell 5%. People Incorporated Form 10-Q, pp. 51–544

Maintaining the sources

Wikipedia and People Inc. illustrate different kinds of exposure. A publisher with syndication deals can earn revenue outside its own pages. A volunteer-maintained reference, a specialist guide or a local reporting outlet may depend on readers for very different things, from corrections and contributions to subscriptions. The next evidence to look for is what happens to those resources after referrals fall. Do publishers still commission original work? Do maintainers still get readers who spot mistakes? A quick answer can be useful today. Its future usefulness depends on someone continuing to do the work it draws on.
