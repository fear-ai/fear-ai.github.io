RESEARCH PREVIEW / ELECTRICITY & ENVIRONMENT

The cloud has
a footprint.

Electricity is physical. “AI demand” is a definition.
Start by separating the two.

FIRST, THE DENOMINATOR.

All computing
isn’t all AI.

Data centers also serve storage, business software and other computing. A company-wide electricity total is another boundary again. These categories can overlap; they cannot simply be added together.

The IEA’s April 2026 Base Case separates all data-center electricity from AI-focused facilities. Both figures below are 2030 projections, not current meter readings. IEA report, Figure 1.5, pp.25–26 ↗

THREE DISTINCTIONS

Read the small print.

01

Power ≠ energy.

A proposed gigawatt connection measures capacity. Annual terawatt-hours measure energy. Turning one into the other needs assumptions about use, ramp-up and timing.

02

A promise ≠ a plant.

A power agreement can cover existing generation, a proposed project or environmental attributes. Its nameplate capacity is not proof of an operating facility.

03

A share ≠ an impact.

A national electricity share cannot settle local questions about water, air quality, noise, bills or infrastructure. Place and time belong beside the annual total.

WHAT WE WANT TO FOLLOW

The company.
The place. The date.

Follow a number from a company disclosure to the relevant region or site. Keep actual consumption, requested grid service and proposed generation separate. Where an AI-only allocation is not disclosed, the honest answer is a visible gap.
